Case Study · Cost & Controls

£1.2M Annual Savings

Bringing a rising cost base under control through restructuring and sharper management reporting.

Challenge

Rising operating cost base

Operating costs had grown ahead of revenue, but the finance function's reporting couldn't say precisely why. Costs were tracked at too high a level to isolate which lines were driving the increase, and pricing hadn't kept pace with the underlying cost-to-serve — so margin was quietly eroding on some of the business's biggest tenders.

Ownership was part of the problem too. Without department-level visibility, cost management sat with finance rather than the operational managers actually incurring the spend, so there was little day-to-day incentive to control it.

Approach

Cost restructuring and management reporting

Restructured the operating cost base from the ground up, reworking the pricing and costing methodology and building a proper stock valuation and ageing system to give real-time visibility on cost of goods. Reviewed the value-for-money process with the supply chain function and introduced additional procurement controls where spend was leaking value.

You can't manage what you can't see by department — visibility comes before discipline.

Rebuilt management reporting so cost drivers were visible by department and service line rather than buried in a single P&L, then delivered cost-management training so operational managers understood and owned their numbers — shifting cost control from a finance exercise to a shared operational discipline.

  • Pricing and costing methodology reworked, used to price tenders worth £30m+
  • Stock valuation and ageing system built and later formalised into software
  • Supply chain value-for-money process reviewed with tighter controls introduced
  • Cost reporting rebuilt by department and service line
  • Cost-management training delivered to operational managers
Result

£1.2M recurring savings

£1,200,000 / year

Recurring annual savings delivered and embedded into the run-rate cost base. Because the new reporting made cost drivers visible on an ongoing basis — not just at the point of the initial review — the savings held rather than drifting back up once attention moved elsewhere.
