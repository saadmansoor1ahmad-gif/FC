Case Study · Capital & Treasury

£26M Funding Programme

Securing strategic capital through consortium-based financing and disciplined treasury management.

Challenge

Capital required for strategic investment

A manufacturing business within a multinational group needed to fund a major asset investment programme — capital equipment and infrastructure to support a period of significant growth. The scale of the requirement meant a single lender wasn't a realistic option, and the business couldn't afford to strain working capital or overleverage the balance sheet to get there.

There was also a timing pressure: the investment window was tied to a wider group growth plan, so funding needed to be structured and secured without delaying the underlying capital projects it was meant to support.

Approach

Structured consortium funding and treasury management

Led the CAPEX budgeting process end to end, working alongside treasury and funding management to size the requirement accurately and phase it against the investment timeline. Rather than pursue a single facility, structured a consortium-based financing arrangement that spread exposure across multiple funding partners — reducing concentration risk and giving the business more flexibility in how drawdowns were phased.

Lenders don't fund a spreadsheet — they fund confidence in the numbers behind it.

To support the application and give lenders confidence in repayment capacity, built a rolling cashflow forecast covering the funding period, and managed the Group's FX exposure in parallel so currency movement didn't erode the funding position during negotiation.

  • CAPEX budget built and phased against the investment timeline
  • Consortium structure negotiated across multiple funding partners
  • Rolling cashflow forecast built to support lender due diligence
  • FX exposure managed throughout the funding process
  • Treasury and covenant positions monitored through drawdown
Result

£26M secured

£26,000,000

Assets worth £26m+ secured for the business, funding the investment programme on schedule while keeping treasury and covenant positions healthy throughout. The consortium structure also left the business better positioned for future funding needs, with established relationships across multiple lenders rather than dependency on one.
